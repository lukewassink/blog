It's probably a little late to write this post. NFT's moment in the sun has come
and gone, and that's probably for the best. But I have strong feelings on this
topic, and now I have a blog ;)

This will (hopefully) be a short post. I will argue that, once we attend
carefully to what ownership and scarcity actually involve, NFTs don't create
either. At best, they give us a somewhat cumbersome mechanism to track ownership
and perhaps an inconvenient for small groups of people who care about them to
communicate ownership.

Very briefly:

- **A blockchain** is simply a way to keep digital records. Think of it like a
    very slow database. It has a few special properties. It's append-only: you
    can add to it, but you can't delete or change existing data. It's
    distributed: it's stored on lots of computers and the official version is
    whatever a sufficient majority of those computers agree on. That means it's
    safe from unilateral changes by any user without requiring a central
    authority to keep track of the official version. Cryptocurrency happens to
    be the major use that blockchains have been put to, but it's not part of the
    definition.
- **A Non Fungible Token (NFT)** is simply a chunk of digital information stored
    on a blockchain. It isn't non-fungible in the sense that it can't be
    precisely duplicated or copied—it easily can—but in the sense that the
    blockchain uniquely associates it with one particular user. It can be
    transferred from one user to another, but it can never be associated with
    multiple users at once.

In the early 2020s there was a lot of excitement around the ideas that NFTs
provide "digital scarcity" or "digital ownership" and that this will somehow
revolutionize the way we interact with the internet. Perhaps particularly the
way digital artists sell and profit from their works.

I believe this is all fundamentally misguided on a basic, philosophical level.
To see why, let's unpack the notion of ownership. We'll ask three questions:

1. What does ownership involve?
1. How does one come to own something?
1. When is it useful to own something?
